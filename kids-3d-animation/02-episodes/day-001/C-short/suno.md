# Suno & sound — "Zip Gets the Hiccups"

No dialogue, so **sound is 60% of this video.** Two layers: a funk bed from Suno, and
hand-placed hiccup SFX that land on the beat.

---

## 1. The bed — Suno

### Style box
```
kids comedy cartoon funk, punchy and silly, bouncy, chunky electric bass, clavinet stabs,
tight funk drums, slide whistle, tuba honks, wood block, big stop-hits and rests,
INSTRUMENTAL, 124 BPM
-vocals -lyrics -ambient -sad -slow -dark -orchestral
```

### Title box
```
Hiccup Boogie
```

### Lyrics box
```
[Instrumental]
```

### What you're listening for

Generate **6 takes** — cheap, and this is a hit-rate game. Pick on one criterion:

> **Does it have big obvious stop-hits?**

You need a track with holes in it — bars where the band drops out for a beat. Those holes
are where the hiccups go. A wall-to-wall groove with no rests is unusable no matter how
good it sounds, because every hiccup will fight the music instead of landing in it.

If none of the six have rests, add `dramatic full-band stop hits, sudden rests, staccato
and space between phrases` and go again.

### Length

Generate ~50 seconds, then find the cleanest **8-bar loop** (about 15.5 s at 124 BPM) and
build the edit on two-and-a-bit repetitions of it. A loop is better than a through-composed
track here because the video itself loops.

## 2. Hiccup SFX — the actual comedy

Each hiccup is a **three-part stack**, placed by hand:

| Layer | Sound | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Intake | short sharp inward gasp / reverse cymbal, 100 ms | on the "and" before the beat |
| 2. **HIC** | a *boing* — slide whistle up, spring reverb hit, or plucked rubber band | **exactly on the downbeat** |
| 3. Result | whatever the physics did: whoosh, splash, thunk, bonk | 2–6 frames after |

Every single hiccup lands on a downbeat. That's the entire trick. Kids don't consciously
notice it; they just find it funnier.

**Sound per beat:**

| Shot | TC | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 02 | 0:01 | gasp + slide-whistle-up + doppler whoosh right |
| 03 | 0:02 | distant hollow *thunk* (off-screen, small, dry) |
| 04 | 0:03 | nothing. Total silence for 1 second under Bibi's stare. |
| 05 | 0:05 | sputtering propeller, wobbling pitch |
| 06 | 0:08 | comedy gulping, three big swallows |
| 07 | 0:11 | gasp + boing + **big water spray whoosh** |
| 08 | 0:13 | single dripping plink. Silence around it. |
| 09 | 0:14 | two tiny soft pats |
| 10 | 0:16 | gasp + boing + **rocket whoosh going up**, pitch rising, then gone |
| 11 | 0:16 | **near silence.** One faint distant whistle, very high, barely audible. |
| 12 | 0:18 | falling whistle down + soft crash |
| 13 | 0:20 | tiptoe pizzicato, one note per step |
| 14 | 0:23 | gasp + boing + **wooden bonk** + tiny sparkle for the impact stars |
| 15 | 0:25 | descending slide whistle, floppy |
| 16 | 0:26 | two boings alternating, in tempo, building |
| 17 | 0:29 | one long slow tuba slide down |
| 18 | 0:31 | gasp + boing + **glass-y ping** for the flying glasses |
| 19 | 0:33 | full-band stop hit. Silence. |

**The silence at shot 04 and shot 11 is doing more work than any of the boings.** Comedy
is contrast; if the whole 35 seconds is loud, nothing is loud.

## Mix

| | |
|---|---|
| Integrated | **−13 LUFS** — Shorts are consumed loud and in noisy rooms |
| True peak | −1.0 dBTP |
| Music bed | −18 dBFS average |
| Hiccup boings | **peak, hot** — these should be the loudest thing in the video |
| Impact SFX | just under the boings |
| Low end | HPF the bed at 80 Hz. Phone speakers can't reproduce it and it eats headroom. |

**Mix on a phone speaker.** Not headphones. This video will be heard almost entirely
through a 12 mm driver, and a mix that's balanced on monitors will have inaudible boings
and a boomy invisible bass line.
