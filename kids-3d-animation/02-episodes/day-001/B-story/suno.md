# Suno & voice — "The Lantern That Wouldn't Light"

This slot needs **three separate audio elements**, not one Suno track:

1. Instrumental score (Suno)
2. Narration (voice)
3. Character dialogue (voice)

Do **not** let Suno sing the story. Narration locked to a melody is the fastest way to
make a five-minute story feel cheap, and it destroys the silences that Act 3 depends on.

---

## 1. Score — Suno, instrumental

Generate **three separate cues** rather than one long track. You need control over where
the music stops, and stopping is the point.

### Cue A — "Lantern Night" (main theme) · 1:30

Style box:
```
cinematic children's storybook score, orchestral miniature, warm and wondrous,
INSTRUMENTAL, celesta, pizzicato strings, solo clarinet, harp, soft timpani, light
wordless choir pad, gentle dynamic build, 82 BPM
-vocals -lyrics -drums -electronic -dark -tense
```
Lyrics box:
```
[Instrumental]
```
Use: 0:18 → 1:12, and the full statement at 4:50.

> Generate this one **first** and pick your favourite melodic motif from it. Everything
> else in the film is a variation on that motif. That's what makes 5 minutes feel scored
> rather than assembled.

### Cue B — "Trying" (montage ostinato) · 1:10

```
cinematic children's score, light comedic ostinato, curious and determined, INSTRUMENTAL,
staccato pizzicato strings, bassoon, wood block, celesta, repeating rising figure,
gently building tension, 96 BPM
-vocals -lyrics -heavy drums -dark -orchestral swell
```
Use: 1:12 → 2:16. In the edit, **pitch each repetition up a semitone** to sell escalation.

### Cue C — "Gogo" · 0:50

```
cinematic children's score, sparse and tender, INSTRUMENTAL, solo clarinet, low sustained
cello, occasional harp, very slow, spacious, warm melancholy resolving to hope, 60 BPM
-vocals -lyrics -drums -percussion -fast
```
Use: 3:30 → 4:35.

### Act 3 — no cue

2:24 → 3:30 is **silence**, or one sustained low cello note you can pull from Cue C's
tail. Do not score this. The absence of music is what makes an 8-year-old lean in, and
it's the single cheapest way to make your channel feel authored.

## 2. Narration

Warm, unhurried, slightly wry. An adult telling a story to a child they like — not a
performance. Roughly **110 words per minute**, which is slower than feels natural.

**Full narration script:**

```
Tonight was Lantern Night in Puddleberry Hollow. And on Lantern Night, everybody carried
a light.

Lanterns in the Hollow don't need a match. You just hold one in your hands, and it
remembers how to shine.

Momo went home to try.

He tried it warm. He tried it still. He tried it the way Bibi did — exactly the way Bibi
did. Twice.

And that is how Momo missed the beginning of Lantern Night.

And Momo's lantern lit. On the tenth try. With four hands — which turns out to be a
perfectly normal number of hands.

In Puddleberry Hollow, nobody ever asks how many hands lit your lantern.

They only ask if you're coming.
```

**Direction:**
- "Twice." — flat, dry, tiny pause before it. It's a joke.
- "And that is how Momo missed the beginning of Lantern Night." — no sadness in the
  voice. Let the picture carry it. Narrating sadness sadly is doubling up.
- "…a perfectly normal number of hands." — warm, almost smiling. This line is for parents.
- "They only ask if you're coming." — slower, then two full seconds of nothing.

If using a TTS/AI voice, pick a mid-warm timbre, disable any "excited/animated" preset,
and hand-edit the pauses. Default TTS pacing is far too fast for this.

## 3. Dialogue

| Character | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Momo** | bright, breathy, slight lisp. Age-4 read. Never whiny — frustrated, which is different. |
| **Bibi** | precise, a beat slower, careful. Genuine delight on "first try!" |
| **Gogo** | very deep, very slow. Leave 2–3 seconds of real silence between his sentences. |

**"Is it?"** — Gogo's two most important words. Not a challenge, not a wink. Genuinely
curious, as if he's also still thinking about it.

## Mix

| | |
|---|---|
| Integrated | −14 LUFS |
| True peak | −1.0 dBTP |
| Score under narration | duck 10 dB |
| Score under dialogue | duck 8 dB |
| Narration EQ | HPF 90 Hz, gentle 3 kHz presence lift |
| Act 3 | let it get genuinely quiet. Don't ride it up. |

Kids' speech comprehension collapses under competing music far faster than adults'. When
in doubt, duck more.
