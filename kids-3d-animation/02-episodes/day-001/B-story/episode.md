# B — "The Lantern That Wouldn't Light"

**Slot:** Storytelling
**Style:** Storybook Cinema (feature-film 3D)
**Runtime:** 5:20 · **Aspect:** 16:9 · **Ages:** 4–8
**Cast:** Momo, Bibi, Grandpa Gogo

**Theme:** asking for help is not the same as giving up.

---

## The idea

Momo's flaw — *tries harder instead of asking* — is the engine. The story never says
"ask for help." It shows a child trying every single thing he can think of, failing,
hiding out of embarrassment, and then learning from an old man that the old man *also*
needed help once. That last part is what makes it land: the lesson arrives as somebody
else's story, not as instruction.

**Hero colour: amber.** Acts 1–4 are graded cool blue-grey. The amber lantern glow does
not appear until 4:47. Deny the colour, then give it.

---

## Script

### COLD OPEN (0:00 – 0:18)

> **No narration. No music.** Just the sound of small paws squeezing paper.

Extreme close-up: two yellow paws wrapped around a small white paper lantern. Squeezing.
Squeezing harder. The paper crinkles. Nothing happens.

Pull back to reveal Momo, alone in a blue-grey dusk, staring at it.

**MOMO** *(small)*: Come on. Come *on.*

TITLE: **The Lantern That Wouldn't Light**

> *Fifteen seconds, the problem is already on screen. Retention data is brutal about
> the first fifteen seconds and a slow scene-setting open is where kids' stories die.*

### ACT ONE — Lantern Night (0:18 – 1:10)

**NARRATOR:** Tonight was Lantern Night in Puddleberry Hollow. And on Lantern Night,
everybody carried a light.

Wide: the Hollow at dusk. Families walking the path, each holding a glowing lantern.
Warm dots of light moving through blue-grey. *Momo is the only dark shape among them.*

**NARRATOR:** Lanterns in the Hollow don't need a match. You just hold one in your hands,
and it remembers how to shine.

Bibi holds hers up. It blooms warm. She grins.

**BIBI:** Momo! Look — mine went on the *first try!*

Momo hides his lantern behind his back.

**MOMO:** Mine too. Nearly.

### ACT TWO — Trying (1:10 – 2:30)

**NARRATOR:** Momo went home to try.

A montage. Each attempt is one shot, and each one is more effort than the last:

1. He holds it very, very still. Nothing.
2. He rubs his paws together fast, then grabs it. Nothing.
3. He warms his paws by the stove until they're pink, and runs to it. Nothing.
4. He squeezes it so hard the paper dents inward, and has to smooth it flat again.
5. He tries with his eyes shut. He tries with one eye open, checking.
6. He puts it on the windowsill in the moonlight and waits, chin on paws.

**NARRATOR:** He tried it warm. He tried it still. He tried it the way Bibi did, exactly
the way Bibi did, twice.

The lantern sits on the sill, white and cold.

**MOMO** *(quietly)*: Why not me?

### ACT THREE — Hiding (2:30 – 3:25)

Outside, the path glows. Voices, laughter, the procession starting.

**BIBI** *(off, calling)*: MOMO! It's starting! Momo?

Momo looks at the door. Looks at the dead lantern. Crawls under the table with it and
pulls his ears down flat over his eyes.

**BIBI** *(off, closer)*: Momo, are you in there?

**MOMO:** …Go away.

A pause. Bibi's shadow at the window. Then it leaves.

**NARRATOR:** And that is how Momo missed the beginning of Lantern Night.

Wide shot from outside: one dark window in a valley full of light.

> *No music at all under this beat. Silence is what makes 4-to-8-year-olds sit forward.*

### ACT FOUR — Gogo (3:25 – 4:35)

A very slow, very deep sound outside. Momo peeks out.

Grandpa Gogo is on the path, not walking to the procession — just sitting. Taking about
nine seconds to finish sitting. His own lantern glows beside him.

**MOMO:** Aren't you going?

**GOGO:** I'm going. I'm just going slowly.

Momo sits down beside him. After a while he shows him the lantern.

**MOMO:** It won't light. I did everything.

Gogo looks at it for a long moment. He doesn't take it.

**GOGO:** Mm. Mine didn't either. First time.

**MOMO:** …Really?

**GOGO:** Sixty years ago. Stood right about here. Squeezed the poor thing half to death.

**MOMO:** So how did you do it?

**GOGO:** I didn't. My sister put her hands round mine.

Beat.

**MOMO:** That's *cheating.*

**GOGO:** *(long pause)* Is it?

He begins the enormous project of standing up. Momo stares at his own paws.

### ACT FIVE — Two Hands (4:35 – 5:20)

Momo runs. Properly runs — the first fast movement in the whole film.

He finds Bibi at the back of the procession, alone, holding her lantern low.

**MOMO** *(out of breath)*: Bibi. Bibi, I need — can you —

He holds out the dead lantern. It's the hardest thing he does all night.

**MOMO:** Will you help me?

Bibi pushes her glasses up. She puts her small blue paws around his.

Close-up: four paws around white paper.

**Nothing happens.**

Then: a single amber spark. Then the whole lantern **blooms** — the first warm colour in
five minutes, and it floods both their faces.

**BIBI:** …Oh.

**MOMO:** *Oh!*

**NARRATOR:** And Momo's lantern lit — on the tenth try, with four hands, which turns out
to be a perfectly normal number of hands.

Wide: the two of them running to catch the procession, one bright light between them.

**NARRATOR:** In Puddleberry Hollow, nobody ever asks how many hands lit your lantern.

Crane up. The whole valley, moving lights, Nana Moon above.

**NARRATOR:** They only ask if you're coming.

END.

## Craft notes

- **Gogo never states the moral.** He tells a fact about himself and asks one two-word
  question. Momo does the work. If you find yourself adding a clarifying line, cut it —
  under-explaining is what separates a story kids rewatch from a lesson they tolerate.
- **"That's cheating." / "Is it?"** is the pivot of the whole film. Give it air: hold on
  Gogo for a full three seconds before he answers.
- **The lantern fails once more at the end.** Four paws, and *still* nothing for a beat.
  That pause is the difference between a satisfying ending and a cheap one.
- **"which turns out to be a perfectly normal number of hands"** — the narrator is warm
  and slightly wry. Parents are in the room. Give them one line.
- Momo never gets the lantern to light alone, at any point, ever. Don't soften it.

## Sound design

- **0:00–0:18** — no music. Paper, breath, one distant lantern chime.
- **0:18** — score enters on the title, warm and curious.
- **1:12–2:16** — montage: a light plucked ostinato that gets slightly more insistent
  with each failed attempt. Same motif, rising a step each time.
- **2:24–3:30** — **drop to nothing.** One low sustained note under Act 3, or silence.
  This is the most important sound decision in the film.
- **3:30–4:35** — solo clarinet for Gogo. Slow, low, unhurried.
- **4:35** — strings enter on the run.
- **4:50** — the full theme, first and only time it plays complete, on the bloom.
- Duck score **10 dB** under all narration.
